## Verdict

Do not freeze this schema yet. The architectural idea—one collaboration identity shown through two role-specific views—is sound. The mistake is treating that as “one table contains the entire lifecycle.”

The `collaborations` row currently acts as application, invitation, negotiation, contract, fulfillment tracker, attribution container, and payment source. That leaks too many rules into callers and RLS. Using the codebase-design lens, keep one collaboration aggregate and place a deep transactional interface around it, but move its independently changing concepts into child tables.

## Findings

1. **P0 — Wallet balances and transactions are forgeable**

Any workspace member can insert, update, or delete any wallet transaction because the policy is `for all` ([schema.sql](/C:/Projects/naano-rebuild/planning/schema.sql:326)). A member could insert a fake top-up or remove a charge. The workspace owner can also directly edit `wallet_cents` through the broad workspace write policy.

There are two competing sources of truth: `workspaces.wallet_cents` and the ledger ([schema.sql](/C:/Projects/naano-rebuild/planning/schema.sql:43), [schema.sql](/C:/Projects/naano-rebuild/planning/schema.sql:208)). They can drift, and concurrent bookings can both pass a balance check.

Proposed change:

- Make ledger entries immutable and server-written only.
- Add `currency`, an idempotency key, and explicit direction—or require positive amounts with transaction-type semantics.
- Add a fund reservation/hold record with `reserved`, `captured`, `released`, and `refunded` states.
- Perform balance check, reservation, and collaboration transition atomically under a locked wallet row.
- Gate both outbound invites and acceptance of creator applications. The latter is currently unaddressed.

2. **P0 — The pricing contract is internally contradictory**

The product promises both fixed-price-per-post and pay-per-qualified-click, but the collaboration only records one `offered_price_cents` ([schema.sql](/C:/Projects/naano-rebuild/planning/schema.sql:152)). There is no CPC rate, click cap, maximum liability, attribution window, or qualification rule.

You cannot determine how much to hold, charge, or pay out. This is the largest unresolved product decision.

Proposed change:

- Decide whether pricing is `fixed`, `cpc`, or `hybrid`.
- Snapshot onto the accepted contract: currency, fixed fee, qualified-click rate, billable-click cap, maximum spend, attribution window, and qualification-rule version.
- Derive `discount_pct`; do not persist it independently from list and offered prices.
- For bundles, snapshot promised deliverables rather than retaining only `offer_type = bundle`.

3. **P0 — “Pipeline attribution” is not actually modeled**

`click_events` can support click counts and a loose company-engagement tile, but not attribution to pipeline ([schema.sql](/C:/Projects/naano-rebuild/planning/schema.sql:182)). A free-text `company` value and `is_qualified` boolean cannot represent an account, opportunity, conversion, pipeline stage, or attributed revenue.

So the spec’s statement that this schema supports “clicks → companies → pipeline” is currently wrong.

Proposed change:

- Treat clicks as immutable raw observations.
- Add qualification status, rejection reason, deduplication key, qualified timestamp, and rule version.
- Normalize companies/accounts with domain and enrichment confidence.
- Add pipeline outcomes—at minimum lead/opportunity/conversion records with value, stage, occurred time, and source.
- Link outcomes to one or more clicks through an attribution table containing the attribution model/version.
- Give creators aggregate analytics, not access to raw company/IP-level data.

4. **P1 — One collaboration identity works; one mutable row does not**

Negotiation currently overwrites `offered_price_cents`. The audit event JSON is the only place previous offers might survive ([schema.sql](/C:/Projects/naano-rebuild/planning/schema.sql:170)). That cannot reliably answer:

- Who made the current counteroffer?
- Which terms were accepted?
- When did that offer expire?
- Was a resubmitted application a new attempt?
- Which bundle version was purchased?
- What terms must a refund or payout use?

Proposed change:

- Retain `collaborations` as the aggregate identity and current read model.
- Add immutable `collaboration_offers` with proposer, terms snapshot, currency, expiry, superseded offer, and acceptance timestamp.
- Point `collaborations.current_offer_id` and `accepted_offer_id` at those rows.
- Add a uniqueness/idempotency rule for active applications per campaign and creator.
- Add deliverable rows for bundles and multiple posts rather than one free-text `deliverables` field.

This preserves “one spine, two views” without forcing every concern into one row.

5. **P1 — The status enum is incomplete, but simply adding more values will not fix it**

Missing lifecycle outcomes include:

- `withdrawn` — sender retracts before acceptance.
- `expired` — `respond_by` passes.
- `revision_requested` — expected consequence of content review.
- `scheduled` — approved but not yet published.
- Potentially `content_in_progress`.

More importantly, offer, fulfillment, and settlement are orthogonal. A collaboration can be published while payment is reserved, or have content under revision while an offer is already accepted. One enum creates invalid combinations and ambiguous states such as `negotiating`, which does not say who must act.

Proposed change:

- Keep a small top-level collaboration phase.
- Put offer status on offers: pending, accepted, declined, withdrawn, expired.
- Put production status on deliverables: drafting, submitted, revision requested, approved, scheduled, published.
- Put money status on reservations/settlements.
- Derive brand and creator pipeline labels through role-specific views.
- Record `action_required_by` and its deadline as an aggregate projection maintained by transition logic.

6. **P1 — Direct table mutation makes safe RLS impractical**

The creator update policy allows a creator to change price, campaign, brief, tracking URL, dates, or status—not merely accept/decline/submit ([schema.sql](/C:/Projects/naano-rebuild/planning/schema.sql:307)). “Constrain columns in app” is not a security control. Likewise, every workspace member can rewrite or delete a collaboration.

This is exactly where RLS gets complicated: authorization depends on the old state, new state, actor, origin, current offer, deadline, wallet reservation, and which columns changed.

Proposed change:

- Make lifecycle tables directly readable but not directly writable by clients.
- Expose a small command interface: `apply_to_campaign`, `invite_creator`, `counter_offer`, `accept_offer`, `decline_offer`, `submit_content`, `request_revision`, `approve`, and `mark_published`.
- Implement those as transactional server operations or carefully secured database functions.
- Validate transitions, ownership, deadlines, cross-workspace references, event creation, and wallet effects inside that interface.
- Keep brand/creator pipeline views as read adapters.

That is the deepest, most testable seam in this design.

7. **P1 — Creator applications are currently impossible through RLS**

Creators have update permission but no insert policy on `collaborations`, so they cannot apply to an opportunity. If direct insert were enabled, they could potentially supply an arbitrary workspace or campaign unless all values were cross-validated.

Relatedly, creators can see an attached collaboration but cannot read its brief because briefs are workspace-members-only ([schema.sql](/C:/Projects/naano-rebuild/planning/schema.sql:300)).

Proposed change:

- Create applications through the command interface, deriving creator and workspace IDs from authenticated user and campaign.
- Require the campaign to be live and open.
- Add a creator-safe brief projection for open opportunities and accepted collaborations.
- Enforce that campaign, brief, and workspace belong together. The current independent foreign keys do not.

8. **P1 — Marketplace RLS exposes creator financial and tax data**

The public creator policy exposes the entire row when `marketplace_visible` is true ([schema.sql](/C:/Projects/naano-rebuild/planning/schema.sql:79), [schema.sql](/C:/Projects/naano-rebuild/planning/schema.sql:293)). That includes tax ID, business-registration status, payout method, bank holder, IBAN last four, and Stripe connection state.

Proposed change:

- Split public marketplace data from private compliance/payout data.
- Put tax and payout details in a creator-private table readable only by the creator and privileged server operations.
- Expose marketplace cards through a deliberately narrow public view.

9. **P1 — Tracking at collaboration level breaks for bundles and multiple posts**

A single `tracking_url` cannot distinguish several posts, link destinations, creative variants, or disabled/reissued links. `/api/track/[collabId]` also couples a public redirect token directly to an internal entity ID.

Proposed change:

- Add `tracking_links` with an opaque public token, destination URL, collaboration, optional deliverable/post, active interval, and campaign parameters.
- Attach each click to a tracking link.
- Keep raw click ingestion server-only and idempotent.

10. **P2 — Audit and relational integrity are too weak for money-bearing records**

Deleting a collaboration cascades into its events, clicks, and posts ([schema.sql](/C:/Projects/naano-rebuild/planning/schema.sql:173)). That destroys evidence needed for billing and attribution. Other correctness gaps include nullable ownership foreign keys, negative monetary values, inconsistent `discount_pct`, mismatched `posts.creator_id`, and an `updated_at` value that never updates automatically.

Also, `conversations`, `messages`, and `referrals` are not RLS-enabled despite the “enable everywhere” comment ([schema.sql](/C:/Projects/naano-rebuild/planning/schema.sql:229)).

Proposed change:

- Soft-delete or archive collaborations; restrict destructive cascades for audit, attribution, and financial records.
- Add nonnegative/range checks and required foreign keys.
- Remove redundant `posts.creator_id` or enforce it against the collaboration.
- Add secure `search_path` and restricted execution privileges to the `SECURITY DEFINER` membership helper.
- Either enable RLS on stub tables now or do not create them yet.

## Recommended model

Keep the architectural slogan, but revise it to:

> One collaboration aggregate, two role-specific views, with immutable offers, deliverables, attribution, and settlement records behind a transactional interface.

The minimum pre-scaffold redesign should introduce:

- `collaborations`: identity and current aggregate projection.
- `collaboration_offers`: immutable negotiation and accepted terms.
- `collaboration_deliverables`: per-post fulfillment.
- `tracking_links` plus richer click qualification.
- Normalized companies and pipeline outcomes.
- Immutable wallet entries plus reservations.
- Private creator compliance/payout data.
- Command-based lifecycle mutations instead of direct client updates.
